# Researching the Week

## Window

Cover news from the seven days before you write. A story from earlier is fine only if something new happened to it this week. Check the publish date on every source, because search results often surface older coverage.

## Where to look

Search engines often surface older coverage first. The fastest route is to open these listing pages directly and scan for this week's dates, then search for anything specific:
- https://www.anthropic.com/news, https://claude.com/blog and the Claude release notes at https://support.claude.com
- https://openai.com/news
- https://techcrunch.com/category/artificial-intelligence/
- https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence

Then search broadly, and confirm on the primary source when you can:
- **Company announcements:** Anthropic (anthropic.com/news, claude.com/blog, support.claude.com release notes), OpenAI, Google (Gemini, DeepMind), Meta, Microsoft, NVIDIA, xAI, Mistral.
- **Tech and business press:** TechCrunch, The Verge, Axios, Reuters, Bloomberg, CNBC, Fortune, The Information, Wired, Ars Technica, The Next Web.
- **Research and data:** SemiAnalysis, McKinsey, Stanford HAI, Pew, academic papers, government reports.
- **Industry press for Dean's readers:** wealth management and RIA coverage (InvestmentNews, ThinkAdvisor, Financial Planning, WealthManagement.com), small-business coverage.

Useful searches: "Anthropic Claude announcement [this week's dates]", "OpenAI launches [month year]", "AI small business [month year]", "AI financial advisors [month year]", "AI regulation [month year]", "AI security breach [month year]".

## Choosing the stories

Aim for a mix like Dean's usual one:
- one Anthropic or Claude story (Dean's readers mostly run on Claude);
- one story from another big lab (OpenAI, Google, Meta, Microsoft, xAI);
- one from the wider world: government and policy, security incidents, law and courts, science, the economy, or a surprising use of AI.

A story earns its place if a business owner would do something differently because of it, or if it reveals something about where AI is heading that they'd want to know. Skip pure benchmark bragging, funding rounds with no wider meaning, and anything a reader can't connect to their own work.

## Choosing the Deep Dive

The best Deep Dives take one thing from the week and use it to explain something lasting:
- a new chart, study or number that reframes a big question (Keynes and the 15-hour week, the 7 spike);
- a news event that exposes how AI behaves (the White House site that looked AI-made);
- the economics of the AI business and what it means for buyers (who loses money on which plans);
- a fight or debate where Dean can weigh both sides and land somewhere (safety researchers who stay).

Check `past-issues.md` so you don't repeat a theme Dean has already done, unless there's a clearly new angle. A callback to a past issue is welcome.

## Choosing From the Field

Look for something a reader can set up this week: a new Claude feature, connector, skill or plan change, or a repeatable workflow built from existing features. Confirm who has access (which plans, which date) and the exact steps from official docs or release notes.

## Verify

- Every number, date, price and name matches the source.
- Each link goes to the article or announcement itself, not a search page or a roundup when the original is available.
- When sources disagree, use the primary one, or leave the detail out.
- If a claim only appears in a single low-quality source, drop it.
- Don't guess at model names or version numbers. Use exactly what the source says.

## Without web search

If you can't search or fetch pages, don't write the issue from memory. Your knowledge of recent AI news will be out of date, and the issue would be wrong in ways Dean's readers would catch. Say so, and ask the person either to turn on web search or to paste in links or notes for this week's stories. If they give you material, write the issue from that material only.
