<One line previewing the Deep Dive, written as a plain-English promise>

## Section notes

### Title
Hooky and specific. Dean's patterns:
- A pop-culture or historical frame: "Is That a Teletubby?", "Ms. Frizzle Takes On the Black Box of AI", "Keynes Promised Us a 15-Hour Week. We Bought Stuff Instead."
- Two short sentences, claim then twist: "Anthropic Loses Money on Its Best Customers. Be One.", "Everybody Building This Thinks It Might Kill Us. Almost Nobody Is Leaving."
- A short phrase the Deep Dive pays off: "Lucky Number 7", "The Permission Slip", "The White House Forgot One Sentence."
Title case. No colons, no "How to", no "The Ultimate Guide".

### "This week:" preview
Exactly three plain lines with no bullets, numbers or bold. The first two read like promises ("Why everyone's AI picks 7 (and what to do about it)", "How to set Claude up to get a little smarter every day without you lifting a finger"). The third crams all the news in ("Anthropic puts 5% of its shares toward the public good, AI helps bring back the dodo, and UK cameras catch 19,000 drivers in two weeks").

### The Deep Dive
One argument, not a roundup.
1. Set up the news, number or idea with enough detail to be credible (who, what, when, the key figures).
2. Explain what it reveals about how AI works or how the AI business works, in plain language with an analogy if it helps.
3. Push back somewhere: on the hype, on the doom, or on Dean's own analogy ("I'll push back on the pile-on a little first.", "I will push back on my own analogy.").
4. Turn to the business: "Now for the business part." What should an owner do differently? Bold lead-ins work well for two to four concrete moves ("**Feed it what only you have.**", "**Ask for the spread, not the answer.**").
5. End on a punchy line or a direct question to the reader, not a summary. Examples: "'We'll see' is the only bad one, because 'we'll see' turns into more email." / "The code is random. Claude picks 7." / "Where do you care, why, and what specifically happens when a mark shows up there?"

A soft call to action is fine at the end of the Deep Dive when it fits: "If you've built something with AI and can't tell whether it smells like AI, send it my way at dean@bridgeaiconsulting.com. I'll tell you honestly."

### From the Field
Practical and specific. Usually a Claude feature, connector or workflow that shipped in the last few weeks, or a repeatable setup built from existing features (a scheduled task, a skill, a review loop). If nothing new this week suits a small or mid-sized business (for example, everything shipped was Enterprise-only), a good evergreen workflow is better than a weak fit. Don't redo a From the Field topic from `past-issues.md` unless there's a clearly new angle, and say so with a callback if you do.
- Headline is often "Statement. Here's the payoff." ("You're Using Claude Like a Search Bar. Here's the Five-Step Fix.", "Claude Can Send Your Email Now. Two Things Before You Let It.") or a short promise ("Teach Claude to Get Better While You Sleep", "Claude Knows Where Your Money Went").
- Say what it is, who has it (plan, date), and exactly how to set it up. Steps can be prose, bold time markers ("**In the morning,**" / "**At night,**"), or bold numbered steps ("**1\. Connect your stuff.**").
- A person stays in the loop. Mention the approval step, the log, or the human review.
- Close with "**Why this travels:**".

### News stories
Three stories (four only in a packed week). Each one:
- **Headline**: a plain, sentence-case statement of what happened, often with a wry edge. "Claude caught China copying its homework", "OpenAI's DevDay: cheaper models, pricier plans", "AI cameras caught 19,000 potential driving offences in two weeks".
- **Summary**: one dense paragraph (two at most), with the date, who did it, the key numbers, and availability or price.
- **My take**: what it means for a business owner and, ideally, one concrete thing to do. Can include a callback to a past issue ("Last month I told you to ask where the cheap models you're being pitched came from. This one at least has an answer.").
- **Link**: all caps, at the end of the My take paragraph, always naming the outlet or company: `[READ THE TECHCRUNCH REPORT →](url)`, `[READ THE ANTHROPIC ANNOUNCEMENT →](url)`. Use ANNOUNCEMENT for company posts, SURVEY or STUDY for research, POST for blogs. Link the primary source (the company's own post, the study itself) when you can open it; otherwise a solid outlet like TechCrunch, Reuters or The Verge is fine, even for all three stories.

### Forward line
Name a specific, slightly funny person tied to this issue's theme. Real examples:
- "the person on your team who writes all your marketing copy with AI and is sure nobody can tell"
- "the person in your company who keeps saying AI is going to give everyone Fridays off"
- "the person on your team who has three AI subscriptions and still asks you which one to use"
- "the person at your firm who is about to ask whether you're allowed to do that"
- "the person on your team who has been quietly worried about all this and has not said so out loud"
