## Fixed skeleton (every issue)

1. **Title**: hooky; often a pop-culture/historical reference or a two-sentence contrarian claim ("X. Y."). Usually no subtitle.
2. **Cold open** (60-130 words, no heading): a scene, joke, stat, question or personal moment, then the thesis in one line. Sometimes names who it's for ("If your team is rationing AI..., this one is for you.").
3. **Optional announcements** (only when real news): bold lead-ins, e.g. "**We're hiring.**", "**Our exec training curriculum is officially out.**", sister-newsletter promo, guest-author note. Introduced like "Before we get there, two announcements I've been sitting on."
4. **"This week:"** then 3 plain lines previewing: the Deep Dive, From the Field, and the news stories (last line lists all news items separated by commas, "and").
5. **The Deep Dive** (H2) > H3 repeating the post title. 450-800 words. One big idea about how AI behaves or the AI economy, turned into a business lesson. Often one chart/image. Bold lead-ins for sub-points ("**First, nobody asked.**", "**Feed it what only you have.**"). Ends with an aphorism or a direct question to the reader, sometimes a soft CTA ("talk to us first", "send it my way at dean@...").
6. **From the Field** (H2) > H3 practical headline (often "X. Here's Y." form). 230-400 words. A concrete Claude workflow, connector, or client story with steps written in prose or bold numbered steps. Humans stay in the loop. Usually ends with "**Why this travels:**" (or "The reason this travels:"), explaining how any business can apply it.
7. **AI News Worth Your Time** (H2) > H3 "Three Stories From This Week" (occasionally four). Each item:
   - H3 headline: plain sentence-case declarative, often witty ("Claude caught China copying its homework").
   - 1-2 factual paragraphs with numbers, dates, named sources.
   - **My take:** 2-4 sentences, business-owner implication, often a concrete action.
   - Link line: `[READ THE <OUTLET> REPORT →]` (or ANNOUNCEMENT / SURVEY / POST), all caps.
8. **Close (near-verbatim every week)**:
   - "That's it for this week."
   - "If this was useful, forward it to [specific, slightly funny persona tied to this issue's theme]. That's who this is for."
   - "And if you want to talk about what AI could do for your business, not in theory, but in your workflows, with your team, on your timeline, reach out at dean@bridgeaiconsulting.com. That's what we're here for."
   - Dean / Founder & CEO, Bridge AI Consulting / bridgeaiconsulting.com
9. **REFERRAL PROGRAM** box (verbatim): "Know a business that could use AI? Refer them to Bridge AI and earn 10% of their first year of payments. Get in touch at dean@bridgeaiconsulting.com to get started."

Total length: ~1,250-1,550 words (Ms. Frizzle outlier ~2,300).

## Voice

- First person "I" for opinions, "we" for Bridge AI, "you"/"your team"/"your business" for the reader.
- Plain-spoken, confident, opinionated, practical. Not hype. Openly pro-Claude ("the one we build on for every client") with disclosure ("I am not neutral here. Bridge AI runs on Claude.").
- Short punchy lines mixed with medium sentences. One-line paragraphs for emphasis ("Nobody cared." "We did not get Fridays." "Everything stops.").
- Dry, understated humor; parenthetical asides ("(long story)", "(In our house, the voice agent has a name. My wife named it Mara.)"); occasional ":)" or ";)", no emojis.
- Pop culture and history used as frames (Ms. Frizzle, Teletubbies, Glinda/Wicked, Keynes, Manhattan Project, NBA, Uber's $6 rides).
- Contrarian takes are standard: "chasing the newest model is the wrong game", "the fix is not a better detector", "pick the one that connects to Claude best, and build the rest yourself".
- Concrete numbers with named sources everywhere. Concrete small-business examples (landscaper, restaurant, drilling-rig rental, RIA, 40-person company).
- Analogies to explain tech ("like an electric meter", "a key is an employee who never sleeps").
- No em dashes. Uses periods, commas, colons, parentheses.

## Recurring themes (the worldview)

- AI defaults to average; your context, taste and data are the differentiator.
- Build your own workflows in Claude before buying another SaaS wrapper.
- Keep humans in the loop (approval steps, review change logs, "every workflow we build at Bridge AI ends with a person").
- Portability and vendor risk: keep prompts/context/workflows movable.
- Buy the right plan and use it hard; cost is falling, so pick tasks well.
- Security hygiene: minimal agent access, API keys, don't give AI your bank password.
- Decide on purpose (policy, saved time) instead of by default.
- Audience: non-technical owners, operators and managers at small/mid businesses; growing tilt to RIAs/wealth management and finance compliance.
- News mix each week: usually one Anthropic/Claude item, one OpenAI/Google/other lab item, one wider-world or risk item (government, security, law, science).
